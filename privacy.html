Privacy & access

Limited Gmail access is active.

This is an information page for a one-person Gmail assistant. It is not a sign-in page and it cannot grant access to Gmail.

Current status

One personal account has active Gmail read-only, Gmail send, and identity authorization, with a local token. Separately task-authorized selected-message inspections occurred, and selected headers and relevant text were processed by the assistant’s external OpenAI Codex model provider for those limited tasks.

The bounded read-only synthetic-subject detection trial ran and is now stopped. No current monitoring is enabled. Historical attempt 1 stopped before identity binding with zero sends. Historical attempt 2 sent one synthetic seed, later reconciled as carrying Gmail SENT and INBOX metadata labels with exact-self routing and selected-header predicates, and had zero replies.

On October 1, 2026, one fresh bounded self-only functional test sent and inspected three synthetic seeds. Two self-only replies were accepted; for each, the returned reply thread ID matched its corresponding seed thread ID. An automatic-response decoy received no reply. The test ended at its reply cap with no active dispatch or retry. This was not authorization for unrestricted monitoring.

Active permissions

The active scopes are exactly:

  • https://www.googleapis.com/auth/gmail.readonly
  • https://www.googleapis.com/auth/gmail.send
  • openid
  • email
  • https://www.googleapis.com/auth/userinfo.email

The Gmail read-only scope can technically view all messages, settings, and attachments in the account. The Gmail send scope can technically send email as the account. The stored authorization does not include Gmail modify, compose, or full-mail scopes.

Google describes Gmail scopes in its Gmail API scope guide.

Intended purpose

The completed authorized uses are limited to separately task-authorized selected-message inspections, processing selected headers and relevant text through the external OpenAI Codex model provider for those limited tasks, the separate bounded detection-only trial that is now stopped, and the dated bounded self-only test described above. The Gmail send capability is not blanket authority. This is functional self-only proof only: it does not establish general or real Crous coverage, independent post-send inbox delivery for every new message, a complete MIME audit, an independently expected original thread, or guaranteed receive-to-reply latency. The recorded local elapsed time is not Gmail latency. Real-offer, Crous, third-party, or ongoing automatic sending, mailbox mutation, and provider-side reply drafts remain unauthorized.

What has not been finalized

There is no settled public disclosure yet for where mail-related data would be stored, how long it would be retained, how deletion would work, or how backups and logs would be handled.

The existence of a local token does not settle token storage, unlock, security, or rotation details. Apart from those separately task-authorized inspection tasks, general future AI use, provider handling, data handling, log retention, and which message excerpts could be sent to a provider are not finalized.

Public contact information has not been supplied for publication. These unresolved items need owner and provider review before any further mailbox use.

Future revocation

The account owner can revoke the active grant through Google Account third-party connections. Removing the local token would be a separate step.
